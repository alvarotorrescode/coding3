# Week 01 – The Anatomy of a Web Page

For this project, I chose Rudyard Kipling’s poem *If—* because it is one of my favourite writing pieces as I perceive it as a message about wisdom, self-reflection and becoming more yourself. I wanted to use the basic HTML and CSS skills I was learning to express what the poem means to me, rather than just putting the text on a page.
![First version of the webpage](documentation/images/change%205.png)

In my first try, the mountain image i wanted to add was too big and it had a watermark, so i searched for another image and a changed its position and size.
![First version of the webpage](documentation/images/version%202%20%20chage%206.png)




In my first version, I used a pale blue background representing the peace and clarity it brings, a red title and a red line beside the poem. I chose different fonts to separate the title and the author from the poem’s message. The red represents the strength of the message, while the overall style tries to communicate the peace and clarity I find in the poem.
![First version of the webpage](documentation/images/fina%20version%201.png)

For the second version, I created a calligram that arranges the words into the shape of a mountain. From a distance, the poem can look like a mountain: something beautiful and complex that you have to climb to understand. For me, reading it is like that climb, because its ideas make you reflect on your insecurities, strengths and the person you want to become.

![First version of the webpage](documentation/images/final%20version%202.png)

For the 3rd verision, i tried to make a more readable layout while keeping the montain image ande pale blue bakcorunda, buy removing the montian shaped text and keeping the  oiginal structure in stanzas. I alos used Arial at 18px, left-aligned paragraphs, 1.5 line spacing and a maximum text width of 65ch to maximize readility.
 This version helped me understand that accessibility does not have to remove the creative concept: it can provide another way to experience the same work.
